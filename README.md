# Heat-Stress in New York City — Policy Paper

**Author:** Descha Daemgen
**Date:** August 21, 2026

A policy analysis of NYC's urban tree canopy and heat-equity efforts under Local Law 148 (the Urban Forest Plan). The paper reviews the institutional landscape — the agencies responsible for the city's trees and the data silos between them — and proposes a set of data science interventions (a "Tree and Heat Atlas" combining LiDAR-derived canopy mapping with Heat Vulnerability Index data, plus a unified Tree Data Dashboard) to target tree planting toward the most heat-vulnerable, under-served neighborhoods.

## Summary

- 23.4% tree canopy citywide as of 2021, unevenly distributed across income and racial lines, against a legal mandate (Local Law 148) to reach 30% by 2035.
- No single city agency is accountable for canopy-wide outcomes; tree, heat, and health data are held in separate systems across Parks, DOHMH, HPD, and other departments.
- Proposes combining NYC's existing 3D LiDAR and tree census data with CNN-based canopy mapping and the city's Heat Vulnerability Index to prioritize planting sites — plus a shared dashboard to address the underlying data-silo problem.
- Short- and medium-term recommendations, including pilot plantings in the South Bronx, East Harlem, and Brownsville, and a citywide target of 15,000–20,000 trees/year directed first to the top decile of heat-vulnerable neighborhoods.
